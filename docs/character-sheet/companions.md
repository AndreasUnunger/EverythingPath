## Companions

Identity and lifecycle are decided by [Decide how companions fit the Character Sheet](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/246). Progression and cross-sheet behavior are decided by [Decide companion progression and cross-sheet calculations](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/249), informed by [companion rules and catalog coverage](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/247) and [progression and combined-form research](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/248). This section is the behavior contract; detailed table shapes and resolver interfaces remain implementation work.

### Coverage and identity

- Launch calculates build-time statistics for animal companions, familiars, cohorts, eidolons and unchained eidolons, including the companion-specific mechanics of in-scope classes and archetypes and their distinct progression or allocation models. Admit their companion rules as needed without adopting unrelated systems from those books. Inputs stay editable and rules checks advisory.
- Each Companion is a Character with its own Character Sheet. Linking an existing Character reuses its identity and recorded choices. Its Character Owner follows the existing rules, initially its creator, and may differ from the associated Character's owner; campaign members retain shared editing.
- Companions appear in the Characters area with their relationship identified and links between the two sheets. The relationship never grants access: do not expose an inaccessible endpoint's name, details or sheet link.
- Keep one unchanged witch spell collection on the witch's sheet. Familiar replacement neither resets it nor splits it into separate familiar collections. This is a deliberate simplification of the companion rules, not permission to delete or reinitialize recorded spells.

### Progression and cross-sheet foundations

- **Companion Progression.** A distinct advancement model supplies actual Hit Dice and progression benefits without inventing Class Levels or broadening fixed Racial Hit Dice into an advancing model. Each rule determines whether progression replaces the creature's baseline Hit Dice; never count the same Hit Dice twice. A familiar's effective Hit Dice remain distinct from its actual Hit Dice.
- **Shared budgets and choices.** Shared advancement budgets and allocations belong to the associated Character's granting features, with each contributing source identified. Species, feats, skill ranks and individual evolutions remain choices on the Companion's sheet; group purchases belong with the shared budget. Dividing effective levels and dividing separate component budgets are distinct operations, as prescribed by each rule.
- **Combined Forms.** A Synthesist's fused statistics appear as a calculated view on the summoner's sheet, linked to the eidolon's build. Preserve separate-form choices and show separate-form results where the rules provide them. Viewing a form neither declares it currently active nor tracks damage, summoning or duration.
- **Familiar hit points.** Half the associated Character's total hit points means half their calculated maximum HP, rounded down and excluding temporary HP. Ordinary HP gained from a temporary Constitution bonus contributes to the current sheet calculation. Existing plain-number HP inputs remain unchanged.
- **Monster cohorts.** Admit the published Bestiary monster cohort equivalence rules and the creature inputs needed to use them. Keep Cohort Equivalence, actual Hit Dice and Class Levels separate; use published mappings where available and an explicitly entered equivalence for an unlisted creature. This does not admit the Monsters as PCs subsystem.

### Calculation inputs and conflicting rules

- **Read the named statistic.** Each companion rule identifies its inputs and operation order. Actual Hit Dice, effective progression level, Class Levels, class-derived BAB and base saves, recorded skill ranks, calculated maximum HP and Combined Form outputs are distinct values. A rule borrowing BAB does not import the associated Character's whole sheet or every attack modifier; likewise, borrowing ranks does not borrow a finished skill total. Read only the components its text supplies.
- **Progression exceptions.** Explicit companion rules override ordinary per-Hit-Die BAB, save, feat and skill-budget derivations. In particular, separately allocated component budgets, such as a Broodmaster's, cannot be reconstructed by applying ordinary formulas to each creature's allocated Hit Dice. Apply replacements, additions, comparisons, caps and rounding in each statistic's prescribed order, without a universal replacement or stacking sequence.
- **Synthesist examples.** Fused BAB replaces only the BAB from summoner Class Levels, retaining BAB from other sources, and evolution effects use the eidolon's Hit Dice ([official FAQ](https://paizo.com/paizo/faq/v5748nruor1fz#v5748eaic9obc)). The Skilled evolution benefits the Synthesist, while a mental Ability Increase to the eidolon does not increase the summoner's mental score ([official FAQ](https://paizo.com/paizo/faq/v5748nruor1fz#v5748eaic9obb)). These specific rules do not justify importing the eidolon's entire statistics or a blanket compatibility ruling for the unchained summoner.
- **Current and permanent calculations.** Current sheet statistics use applicable current inputs. Militia Character Facts use the ordinary form and exclude Temporary Effects at every step across linked Characters. The existing retroactive permanent-Int policy remains wherever a skill budget uses Intelligence; it does not invent an Intelligence contribution to a rule-defined fixed or allocated budget. HP inputs remain plain recorded numbers, without automatic rolls, averages, maximization or prefilling.
- **Competing rules.** Apply published precedence automatically. Where applicable rules prescribe incompatible calculations without precedence, let the player choose and save the interpretation or an explicit fallback for the affected value, while retaining compatible contributions. Never choose by newest entry or discard unrelated contributions. Until the choice or fallback supplies the missing answer, that value is visibly unresolved.
- **Shrinking budgets and source loss.** Preserve recorded allocations and recalculate from those choices when a shared budget shrinks. An over-budget allocation raises an advisory warning; never trim or redistribute it automatically. Choices exclusively dependent on a vanished Grant follow the existing dormancy and Keep rules. Remove only the lost source's contribution, preserve surviving contributions and choices, and treat truly unavailable inputs as unresolved rather than inventing replacement statistics. Losing one source does not interrupt a relationship that still has rules support.

The #317 input mechanism lives in `src/lib/character-sheet-linked-inputs.ts`. The resolver distinguishes available, unavailable and conflicting inputs, returns surviving source contributions, and applies published precedence or a saved interpretation before an explicit fallback. Disabled or no-longer-counting supporting sources are excluded from candidates. If the preferred source is lost, agreeing surviving alternatives calculate automatically; conflicting survivors require an available saved interpretation. A still-counting source with an unavailable numeric input remains a missing dependency unless published precedence or a saved interpretation resolves it. An unresolved value remains `null`.

Relationship creation, linking and source addition accept recorded support choices, never player-authored calculation bindings or contribution roles. Ordinary sheet reconciliation preserves this declaration, so hidden sheet edits cannot disclose live class changes. ### Required catalog support

- Catalog definitions and curated resources must carry the source rules, progression tables, species inputs, eidolon subtypes, version-specific evolutions, familiar base-creature inputs and admitted monster cohort mappings needed by the supported builds. Keep original and unchained rules distinct where their mechanics differ. Imported templates or prose alone do not establish working automation.
- Missing rules structures and calculation inputs must be visible as unresolved values, with the saved fallback behavior below. A fallback keeps the sheet usable but does not satisfy the promised rules coverage: missing curated structures remain required work. Do not silently freeze a previous result, substitute zero or claim a build is calculated from an imported description alone.
- Existing Attribution Assessments, holds and retained-exception treatment apply to all companion definitions and resources. Admission of companion rules neither bypasses the notice gate nor turns retained held content into permission for new selections or Grants.

### Relationships and restoration

- A Companion has at most one active associated Character. An associated Character may have multiple Companions, and multiple supporting sources may contribute according to the rules. Former relationships remain inactive.
- A relationship can be active only when both Characters share a campaign, or both are private with the same Character Owner. Moving apart or losing the rules support for the relationship makes it inactive while preserving the Companion's sheet and choices. Losing one contributing source alone preserves surviving contributions and does not interrupt a still-supported relationship. Replacing a Companion also retains the former relationship and sheet.
- Automatically resume an interrupted relationship when its rules support or compatible campaign/private arrangement returns. An explicitly replaced relationship requires player reselection; restoration never displaces a newer relationship.
- Block self-links and active relationship cycles as data-integrity errors, while retaining inactive history. Automatic restoration that would create a cycle stays inactive instead of evicting another relationship.

Creating a Companion accepts a separate optional Character kind (`pc` or `npc`), defaulting to `npc`; relationship kind never dictates Character kind. Private creation inherits the associated Character’s prepared/demo marker and requires that Character’s existing prepared write access. A newly created Companion is not added to the militia roster or assigned an officer role.

Prepared relationships are stored in `companionRelationship` and discovered in both directions through `companionRelationships:list`. Each supporting source has a durable key and either an explicit recorded support flag, a Character Sheet Entry reference, or a Grant Key. Referenced support follows effective counting, including dormant entries that are Kept; Class Level references follow their active recorded level. Base scores, Ability Damage and Ability Drain cannot grant relationship support. An explicit player interruption preserves source choices and resumes only through Restore; rule-support and endpoint-arrangement interruptions resume automatically. Read DTOs report source availability independently of its recorded enabled flag.

The resolver preserves eligible active edges before considering interrupted relationships, so restoration cannot evict an active association or close a cycle. Sheet writes, ownership reassignment, archive writes and deletion reconcile stored relationship status in their transaction; reads also derive eligibility from current endpoint ownership, campaign placement and support. Archive state does not remove independent sheet access or interrupt an otherwise compatible relationship. Reconciliation attributes status transitions and changed referenced supporting-source availability to the initiating operation, including automatic side effects, without changing unrelated rows’ attribution. Replacement and support edits require access to the associated Character; activation also requires access to the Companion. Restore rechecks current supporting-source availability, access, conflicts and cycles before reporting success. A replaced relationship must be reselected before it can replace another Companion. The former Companion can remain inaccessible or deleted during replacement, and its retained endpoint is disclosed only when independently accessible. Rows with an inaccessible endpoint omit relationship status and the last operation ID. Reverse rows with an inaccessible associated Character omit supporting-source details and the interruption reason, while retaining only curated linked-input declarations so fallback editing remains reachable. Deletion retains relationship history and never deletes another Character Sheet. The indexed connected graph is bounded at 1,024 Characters and Relationships, including inactive history; exceeding either bound returns a clear error.

Prepared movement (#711) extends the #316/#703 machinery to recursively active, same-owner Companions. Eligibility uses effective relationship status, including current rules support and endpoint placement. Inactive links and different-owner links stop traversal; moving a Companion never pulls its associated Character along. A group contains at most 16 Characters including the requested Character, matching the downstream fact-refresh bound. A larger group must be reduced by interrupting a relationship before moving; a resource refusal never publishes a smaller subset.

`characterMoves.preview` authorizes the requested Character's current owner before discovering the group. It returns moving Characters and staying endpoints with `inactive`, `differentOwner` or `associated` reasons. Independently inaccessible staying endpoints have no name or Character ID. `useCharacterMove` exposes `groupPreview`, waits for it before enabling Start, and hides cached group/campaign details on refused reads. Progress aggregates preparation; `completedMembers` remains zero until every Character publishes together.

Each Character prepares its complete dependency closure through the existing bounded discovery/staging workflow. Final publication rechecks all owners, ownership-operation tokens, campaigns, effective group links and destination access, then remaps all sheets and moves the group in one transaction. Only after all campaign transitions are selected does it refresh linked facts and reconcile interruptions/restoration. Saved choices, identities and independent statistics stay intact; separation preserves inactive history, automatic restoration never replaces a newer active relationship, and replaced history still needs player reselection. A changed eligible group fences preparation with `groupChanged`, leaving every Character in place. These prepared implementations do not activate production Character Sheets or companion calculations.

### Prepared animal Companion Progression (#325)

`CharacterSheetInput.companionProgression` is the Companion's recorded state, stored as optional `base.state.companionProgression`: `{ species: 'wolf' | 'horse', hpGained: number | null, skillRanks: Record<string, number>, abilityIncreases: Ability[], advancementChoice: 'species' | 'abilityScores', keep: boolean }`. Species and individual ranks/ability choices stay on the Companion. Setting or linking this state preserves actual Class Levels; no placeholder Class Level represents advancing HD. HP remains a recorded plain number without roll/average/max prefills. Species statistics form a separate calculation layer; every save preserves the Character’s recorded ability scores.

`ResolveOptions.companionProgressionSources` identifies each supporting contribution with `{ sourceKey, ruleKind, counting }`, where the representative rule kinds are `animalCompanion`, `druidCompanion`, `rangerCompanion` and `cavalierMount`. `companionLinkedInputs` supplies the precise authorized granting-class inputs. Prepared reads derive these from retained relationships, independently resolve ordinary/current and permanent inputs, and pass the same inputs to prerequisite checks. An available source's values suspend saved fallbacks; an unavailable input may use its explicit saved fallback or remain unresolved. Source keys join provenance internally and are never player-facing labels.

The representative catalog in `src/lib/catalog/representative-animal-companions.ts` contains the complete CRB Table 3–8 levels 1–20 and Wolf/Horse starting statistics, with seventh-/fourth-level species advancement, the alternative Dexterity/Constitution advancement, source citations, and ranger/cavalier source restrictions. Effective level combines compatible granting-class contributions before one table lookup; duplicates never count the same family twice. Ranger contributes its class level minus three. The table's HD replace baseline creature HD rather than adding them or advancing a fixed racial progression; linked Characters keep their actual Class Levels and fixed racial metadata separately. This is representative prepared coverage, not a claim to the full animal species/archetype catalog.

`calculateCharacterSheet(...).companionProgression` returns a source-backed resolution: `status` (`active`, `dormant`, `unresolved`), species, nullable effective level and progression HD, size, `advancement: { level, applied, choice }`, contributions, budgets (`generalFeats`, `skillRanks`, `abilityIncreases`), bonus tricks, features, source citations, restrictions and retained-choice state. Contributions identify their class family and source and carry `counting`, nullable class/effective levels, input `resolution` and `fallbackState`. The main calculation applies progression BAB/base saves, natural armor, ability changes, recorded HP, skill/rank/feat/ability budgets and actual HD; explicit companion budgets replace ordinary per-HD assumptions. Animal granting-class inputs supply effective progression only; they never satisfy the companion’s own Class Level prerequisites. Current and recorded-level prerequisites read the companion’s own recorded rank totals and mount proficiency grants. Named linked skill reads likewise use recorded ranks, including a classless Animal Companion, without borrowing its finished skill total. Unavailable advancement keeps required BAB, HD, HP and physical-score dependencies unresolved. An unresolved general feat budget omits its comparison slot rather than warning against zero. Lost contributions stay addressable, surviving sources continue to count, and budget warnings do not trim recorded choices. Losing all support preserves choices and independent statistics while dependent totals remain unresolved where no residual rule is supplied; Keep preserves chosen ability increases and recorded ranks without inventing a previous source level or HD table result. Dormant and unresolved progression budgets remain null.

`characterSheet.setCompanionProgression` accepts `{ ...scope, operationId, progression: state | null }` through the existing prepared writer guard and campaign/private authorization. A null record intentionally removes progression choices; interruption never calls that removal. The mutation validates structural state and bounded ranks/ability selections, preserves rule mismatches as warnings, records the sheet change, and reconciles relationship/fact changes transactionally. Production's legacy Character authority and initial-cutover gate remain unchanged.

Public reads also return nullable `companionCalculationInputs`: for a progression companion, `{ kind: 'progression' }` with the exact viewer-filtered current animal `companionLinkedInputs` and `companionProgressionSources`. Linked-input DTO parsing strips command attribution and display-only metadata; no trusted inputs enter this public response. The Situation preview uses these inputs with the recorded base progression and sheet mode, preserving unavailable/conflicting/fallback semantics and species statistics while applying selected Situations. Nested master DTOs stay internal.

The public sheet controller exposes `useCharacterCompanions(...).progression`, backed by `useCharacterCompanionProgression`. It returns a read summary, species choices, `save`, local status, loading/maintenance/disabled state and dismissible remote-change feedback. `buildCompanionProgressionView(snapshot)` maps the supplied ordinary and permanent results, recorded choices, source labels, applied/suspended fallback states, advancement and warnings; it never recalculates rules in the presentation layer. It labels actual HD, actual Class Levels and effective progression separately, and exposes unresolved dependent numeric summaries as null. JSX and responsive presentation are handed off through `/tmp/ep-orch/briefs/325/ui-contract.md`.

Prepared Militia Character Facts add optional `actualHitDice: number | null` when Companion Progression exists, so source rules that replace baseline HD do not require a negative increment or double-count fixed racial metadata. The reserved computed-HD Ruleset Version uses a roster's explicit override first (zero included), then this actual total, then Class Levels plus fixed racial HD when the total is absent. A null total preserves unavailable progression without claiming a calculated value. #423 adds the officer requirements for missing statistics and the `unresolvedAbilities` snapshot field (see [Ordinary companion facts in militia weeks](#ordinary-companion-facts-in-militia-weeks-423)). Version 9 and historical snapshots without `actualHitDice` retain their previous readings. The reserved version remains unactivated during preparation.

### Prepared eidolon progression (#409)

The original summoner's eidolon extends the #325 Companion Progression seam rather than adding a parallel one. `base.state.companionProgression` is now either the animal record or an eidolon record `{ kind: 'eidolon', baseForm: 'biped' | 'quadruped' | 'serpentine', size: 'medium' | 'small', hpGained, skillRanks, abilityIncreases, classSkills, armorBonusAsArmor, evolutions: { key, choice?, upgrades? }[], keep }`. Stored animal records keep their shape, so no backfill is needed. Base form, individual evolutions, ranks, chosen class skills and ability increases stay on the Companion. Unknown evolutions, choices and upgrades are structural errors; every rules mismatch is an advisory warning.

`src/lib/character-sheet-companion-sources.ts` holds the shared supporting-source and contribution helpers. A progression reads only its own rule kinds: animal sources never advance an eidolon, and eidolon sources (summoner class family) never advance an animal. Both eidolon relationship kinds supply sources; since #418 each source's rule kind follows the version of the summoner levels it reads, as described below.

[The representative eidolon catalog](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-eidolons.ts) curates APG Table 2–9 for summoner levels 1–20, the three base forms with their free evolutions and the Small option, and the complete APG evolution list with costs, summoner-level minimums, base-form limits, required evolutions, repeat rules, choices, upgrades and natural attacks. Text was checked against the [PRD summoner page](https://legacy.aonprd.com/advancedPlayersGuide/baseClasses/summoner.html). The PRD prints no page numbers, so the table (p. 57) and base form (pp. 57–58) citations follow the ticket; the evolution pages (58–63) were not verified. Ultimate Magic evolutions are out of scope; Broodmaster component allocation, Master Summoner's reduced progression and the Synthesist's Combined Form are described [below](#prepared-component-allocations-419).

The summoner level keys one table lookup. The table's HD replace baseline creature HD; linked Characters keep actual Class Levels and fixed racial metadata separately. The resolver applies BAB, good and bad saves by base form, the Str/Dex bonus, recorded HP plus Constitution per HD, and the ability increases at summoner levels 5, 10 and 15. Skill ranks are 6 + permanent Int modifier per HD, capped at HD per skill. The feat budget is the table's Feats column, which equals the ordinary HD formula. The table armor bonus becomes natural armor unless `armorBonusAsArmor` assigns part of it as an armor bonus; a recorded split above the table calculates the table maximum and warns.

Evolution effects calculated on the sheet:

- Ability Increase (+2; it costs 4 for Strength or Constitution of a Large or Huge eidolon).
- Improved Natural Armor (+2).
- Skilled (+8 racial).
- Large or Huge size, scores, natural armor and Fly/Stealth size modifiers. Huge replaces Large rather than stacking.
- Small base form adjustments.
- Weapon Training proficiencies.
- Flight makes Fly a class skill.

Other evolutions are calculated for cost, requirements and attack count; their effects stay prose summaries. Natural attacks list each type's count, role and printed dice for the eidolon's size, together with how many matching natural attack entries are recorded. Evolutions do not create entries: the player adds them through the #323 natural attack form. From summoner level 9, an eidolon with three or more natural attacks gains Multiattack for Attack Routines (`builtin:eidolon:multiattack`).

`calculateCharacterSheet(...).companionProgression` is now a union discriminated by `kind` (`animal` or `eidolon`). The eidolon result adds:

- `evolutionPool { total, spent, remaining, sources[{ sourceKey, ruleKind, classRuleIdentity, points }] }`: the pool and its provenance on the counting supporting source.
- `evolutions[]`, with cost, `free` for base-form evolutions, `missingChoice` and `issues`.
- `naturalAttacks[]`, `attackCount` and `multiattack`.
- `armor { total, armor, natural }` and `strengthDexterityBonus`.
- `budgets.maxAttacks`. The evolution pool is not a Companion budget: it appears only in `evolutionPool`, on its supporting source.

Budgets are null while progression is dormant or unresolved.

Over-budget spending, too many natural attacks, unmet requirements, missing choices, more than four chosen class skills, an armor split above the table and base scores different from the base form's each warn. None trims or redistributes a recorded choice. A summoner level drop shrinks HD and the pool, keeps every evolution calculating and warns; restoring the level restores the same results. Losing one of two supporting sources keeps the summoner contribution, and its pool moves to the survivor. Losing all support makes progression dormant: Keep preserves ability increases, ranks and evolution effects without inventing table values. An unavailable summoner level stays unresolved until a saved fallback applies. Ordinary and permanent projections read the summoner level separately and differ only through Temporary Effects. Militia facts read the eidolon's actual HD.

`characterSheet.setCompanionProgression` accepts the eidolon record unchanged. Public reads return `companionCalculationInputs` for eidolons as for animals. `useCharacterCompanions(...).progression` adds `eidolon` (`buildEidolonProgressionView`), `eidolonBaseForms`, `evolutionCatalog` and `newEidolon(baseForm)`. JSX is handed off through the #409 UI contract.

### Prepared Unchained eidolons (#418)

The Unchained eidolon extends the same eidolon record and resolver rather than adding a parallel kind. The record gains three optional fields: `version: 'original' | 'unchained'` (absent means original, so #409 records need no backfill), `subtype` and `subtypeChoices: { key, choice }[]`. The calculation follows the recorded version. The Unchained subtype and its choices stay on the record under either version.

[The representative Unchained catalog](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-unchained-eidolons.ts) is curated apart from the APG one. Text was checked against the [legacy PRD Unchained summoner page](https://legacy.aonprd.com/unchained/classes/summoner.html), which prints no page numbers, so pages follow Archives of Nethys' source lines. The catalog has:

- **Eidolon Base Statistics** for levels 1–20. Every column matches APG Table 2–9 except the Evolution Pool, which equals the HD column: smaller than the APG pool, because the subtype supplies evolutions of its own. AoN prints no page for this table; the PRD prints it between the class features (p. 25) and the subtypes (p. 29), so its page range (pp. 25–29) is unverified.
- **The twelve subtypes printed in the book (pp. 29–34):** agathion, angel, archon, azata, daemon, demon, devil, div, elemental, inevitable, protean and psychopomp. Each has its alignment, the base forms it allows with their free evolutions, its fixed evolutions by summoner level (including resistances that become immunities at 16th), its pool points, its damage reduction and prose features. Subtypes from later books, such as kami, are not curated.
- **The base forms (p. 34).** Their scores, saves and speeds are the APG ones.
- **The Unchained evolution list (pp. 35–39).** It has every APG evolution, with these differences: subtype requirements; Pounce costs 3 points from 7th level; Constrict needs Grab; Poison needs a bite or sting; Damage Reduction raises the subtype's DR by 5 from 15th level, with no alignment choice or DR 10 upgrade; Large and Huge give +4/+2 and +8/+4 Str/Con.

These curated values were checked against the PRD text: the subtypes' pool points (archon and elemental +1 at 4th; daemon, demon, div and psychopomp +1 at 8th); the archon's Skilled (Intimidate) until 16th; the protean's two grabs (its base form's grab on the tail slap and a 1st-level grab on an attack type the summoner chooses); Pounce (a 3-point evolution, summoner level 7th); Damage Reduction (3 points, summoner level 15th and the subtype's damage reduction); and Large (+4 Str, +2 Con, +2 natural armor, summoner level 8th) and Huge (+8 Str, +4 Con, +5 natural armor, 13th). Their printed page numbers are unverified.

The subtype chooses the base form's free evolutions. A missing subtype, or a base form the subtype does not offer, falls back to the plain base form's and warns. Fixed subtype evolutions appear in `evolutions[]` with `origin: 'subtype'`, their `level` and zero cost. They are non-removable, and they meet requirements and add effects such as Skilled, Ability Increase, Weapon Training and Flight's Fly class skill. Some subtype evolutions ask for a choice: the elemental's element, the archon, azata, demon and psychopomp Ability Increases, and the protean's 1st-level Grab. Each choice is recorded under its key in `subtypeChoices`; the catalog names the subtype each key belongs to (`unchainedSubtypeChoiceOptions[key].subtype`), and a fixed evolution row carries the `choiceKey` it asks for, so no layer reads a key's spelling. A missing choice warns (`eidolonSubtypeChoiceMissing`) and supplies no effect. Subtype pool points join `evolutionPool.total` and are listed in `evolutionPool.bonuses`; the source still shows the table's points.

**Versions and mixing.** Pathfinder Unchained has one Character use one version of a class exclusively (ch. 1 introduction), and the Unchained summoner's eidolon is the subtype-based Unchained eidolon (p. 25). So `convex/lib/companionRelationshipGraph.ts` derives each eidolon source's rule kind from the summoner version it reads:

- A Class Level source takes that level's version.
- A Grant source takes the version of the summoner Class Level that grants it.
- Any other source takes the version of the sheet's summoner levels when they are all one version, and otherwise the relationship kind.

Original levels give `eidolon` and Unchained levels give `unchainedEidolon`. An eidolon counts only sources of its own version. Sources of the other version stay listed in `contributions` without counting. If only other-version sources would count, the progression is `unresolved` with `versionMismatch: true` and an `eidolonVersionMismatch` warning, and every choice is kept. Saved summoner-level fallbacks do not bridge versions. Switching the summoner's class version never edits the Companion: the player converts the eidolon explicitly by saving its version through `setCompanionProgression`, and switching back restores the same results.

**Version-specific choices.** These stay recorded and visible but do not count:

- A recorded evolution choice or upgrade the current version does not offer, such as the APG Damage Reduction alignment or DR 10 upgrade on an Unchained eidolon. It is listed in that evolution's `dormant` parts and adds no cost or effect.
- An Unchained subtype and its choices on an original eidolon.
- A former subtype's choices after a subtype change.

`dormantChoices` lists the subtype state with its `reason` (`version` or `subtype`), and `eidolonVersionUnavailable` warns about version-unavailable state. Restoring the version or subtype restores the same choices. There is no per-item Keep for this state, because the other version has no rule to apply it to; the GLOSSARY calls it a Version-unavailable choice, distinct from a Keepable Dormant entry. Subtype requirements are ordinary advisory requirement issues, so a recorded bite on an angel keeps calculating and warns.

**Archetypes.** An Archetype written only for the original summoner, active on an Unchained summoner, is never assumed to change the Unchained eidolon. The associated sheet's reader passes its name on the eidolon's sources (`unsupportedArchetypes`, disclosed only with access to the associated Character). The eidolon lists it and warns with `eidolonUnchainedArchetype`. Master Summoner is among them: an Unchained eidolon reads its whole summoner level and lists Master Summoner unsupported (#420). So is Synthesist: an Unchained summoner's Combined Form is never assumed (#421).

`characterSheet.setCompanionProgression` accepts the extended record; there is no new writer. Unknown subtypes, subtype choice keys and options are structural errors. A record that omits `version` on a Companion with an `unchainedEidolon` relationship is saved as `unchained`, as the UI's first record is, so it never calculates as an original eidolon; elsewhere an omitted version stays original. `useCharacterCompanions(...).progression` adds `evolutionCatalogFor(version)`, `eidolonVersions`, `unchainedSubtypes` and `newEidolon(baseForm, version)`. Components apply the pure record edits (`withEidolonVersion`, `withEidolonSubtype`, `withEidolonSubtypeChoice`, `withoutEidolonDormantChoice` and the evolution edits) from the view model directly. `buildEidolonProgressionView` adds the version, mismatch, subtype, dormant choices, unsupported Archetypes, fixed-evolution labels and other-version contribution labels. An Unchained eidolon relationship now shows the eidolon block, and its first record is Unchained. JSX is handed off through the #418 UI contract.

### Prepared effective-level allocations (#410)

Beast Master divides one effective druid level among several animal companions before each companion's progression lookup. This is level allocation, not the separate component allocation of a Broodmaster (#419). The budget and its allocations belong to the associated Character's granting feature; the Companion keeps its species and individual choices.

[The cited rule declarations](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-companion-rules.ts) curate three allocation sources and one increase. Each was checked against Archives of Nethys; the legacy PRD pages print no page numbers, so the pages follow AoN's source lines.

| Source                | Supporting rule kind   | Budget or effect                                                                                                  | Citation                                             |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Beast Master (ranger) | `beastMasterCompanion` | Ranger level − 3; Strong Bond makes it the ranger level from 12th. Any species a druid can choose.                | Advanced Player's Guide p. 124                       |
| Pack Lord (druid)     | `packLordCompanion`    | Druid level                                                                                                       | Ultimate Magic p. 38                                 |
| Packmaster (hunter)   | `packmasterCompanion`  | Hunter level                                                                                                      | Advanced Class Guide p. 96                           |
| Boon Companion (feat) | `boonCompanion`        | +4 effective levels for one companion per Selection, to at most character level; never supports a companion alone | Ultimate Wilderness p. 217; Seekers of Secrets p. 16 |

A supporting source gets one of these rule kinds from its Archetype Selection, one of that Archetype's feature Grants, or a Class Level of a class with that Archetype active and applicable. The feat source gets its kind from the Boon Companion Selection. These kinds apply only to animal companion relationships; for other relationship kinds the ordinary derivation is unchanged.

**Allocation and lookup.** `companionRelationship.sources[].allocatedLevels` records one companion's share: a whole number from 0 to 20 that the player sets on the relationship's source. The source contributes its recorded share, not the whole budget, as that companion's effective level. Other compatible sources then stack onto the companion they support, as CRB p. 51 says effective druid levels from several sources stack. A druid 3 source and a Beast Master share of 3 give effective level 6. Stacking druid levels onto the companion a share supports, rather than dividing them too, is the chosen interpretation: CRB p. 51 is general and #248 leaves only the allocation order unworked. Boon Companion applies last: it adds up to four levels without taking the total above the associated Character's level.

A counting allocation source replaces the ordinary contribution of its own class family whatever the source order, so a ranger's hunter's bond and its Beast Master share never both count. The research example holds: a level-7 Beast Master gives one companion 4 HD with all four levels, or two companions 3 HD each with two levels each.

**Provenance.** Each contribution keeps its `sourceKey`, `ruleKind`, class levels and input resolution, and its `kind` says how it counts: `ordinary`, `allocation` or `increase`. Allocation contributions add `allocation { levels, budget, pool }`; increases add `increase { amount, characterLevel, pool }`. `pool { allocated, companions, selections }` totals the counting shares of that rule kind across the associated Character's active relationships, one source per relationship; `selections` counts the distinct Selections or Grants behind them. `pool` is null unless the viewer can open the associated Character. The resolver's restrictions name each allocation or increase rule; Beast Master lifts the ranger's species limit, so the hunter's-bond restriction and ranger level 4 warning do not apply to it.

**No trimming, no winner.** An over-budget share keeps calculating at its recorded level and raises `companionAllocationBudget` on every counting companion of that budget. The same warning reports Boon Companion Selections that support more companions than there are Selections: each time the feat is taken it applies to a different companion (UW p. 217), so two Selections on two companions are fine and one Selection on two companions warns. A second Boon Companion source on the same companion never stacks. A missing share raises `companionAllocationMissing` and leaves progression unresolved. A shrinking budget, such as ranger levels being removed, keeps every share; restoring the levels clears the warning. Disabling or losing a Beast Master source removes only its contribution: surviving sources still count, the share stays recorded on the source, and it no longer counts toward the budget. Interrupted and replaced relationships keep their shares but do not count. A replacement relationship copies the replaced relationship's sources, so its share comes with it; the replaced share stops counting. APG p. 124 forbids redistributing an allocated level while that companion is in the ranger's service; moving a share between serving companions is not yet warned.

**Fallbacks.** An allocation contributes only while its budget input (the class levels) resolves. A hidden associated Character leaves the companion unresolved, and the budget totals stay hidden. A saved fallback for that class level resolves the share, and restored access suspends the fallback without deleting it.

**Writers and reads.** `companionRelationships.setSourceAllocation({ relationshipId, sourceKey, levels: number | null, operationId })` is a new legacy Character writer. It requires write access to the associated Character, as the other source edits do. It records or clears one share, refuses non-integers and values outside 0–20 with the same schema the stored progression source and the editor use, and refuses shares on sources that do not divide levels; clearing is always allowed. It refreshes the source's stored rule kind and the relationship's `lastOperationId`, reconciles relationships and refreshes the companion's militia facts in the same transaction. The latest write wins and is attributed; nothing is merged. Link and add-source writes cannot set a share. `companionRelationships.listAllocationPools({ characterId })` returns the associated Character's budgets with their citations and restriction text; its validator derives from the shared pool schema. Each pool has a `kind`. An `allocation` pool lists its class levels, budget, allocated, remaining and over-budget state, plus every member's share and counting state. An `increase` pool's budget is one companion per distinct Selection, and its members have no share. Inaccessible companions appear only as "Character unavailable", with no name, link or operation ID.

**Client.** `useCharacterCompanions(...).allocations` (`useCharacterCompanionAllocations`) opens one share editor at a time. The editor prefills the recorded share, or the unallocated remainder. It uses `react-hook-form` and `zod` with distinct empty and malformed messages, keeps refused drafts, gives local save feedback per share and disables writes during maintenance. Another device's share change raises a dismissible notice without replacing an open draft. `buildCompanionAllocationPoolView` words each pool; the Companion's progression view adds `allocationSummary` to each contribution. The relationship write status also compares `allocatedLevels`. JSX is handed off through the #410 UI contract.

Representative browse-only seeds add Druid, Ranger and Hunter classes (no feature schedules) and the Beast Master, Pack Lord and Packmaster Archetypes with their added features, plus the Boon Companion feat ([representative archetype notes](../catalog-import/archetypes.md)).

### Prepared component allocations (#419)

A Broodmaster looks up one undivided row of the eidolon table and divides its separate components among a brood of eidolons. This is component allocation, not the level allocation of #410: an eidolon's share is a set of recorded outputs, not a level, so nothing reconstructs its statistics from its allocated Hit Dice or reads them through the effective-level lookup.

[The cited rule declaration](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-companion-rules.ts) curates Broodmaster as the `broodmasterEidolon` supporting rule kind, from Ultimate Magic p. 78 ([legacy PRD summoner archetypes](<https://legacy.aonprd.com/ultimateMagic/spellcastingClassOptions/summoner.html#broodmaster-(archetype)>); the PRD prints no page, so the page follows Archives of Nethys' source line). The text was checked against both. A summoner source gets this rule kind from the Broodmaster Selection, its feature Grants, or an original summoner Class Level with the Archetype active. The calculation lives in `src/lib/character-sheet-companion-components.ts` and the eidolon resolver.

**Whole and divided statistics.** "Each eidolon has the same base attack bonus and base saving throw bonuses", so BAB and saves read the whole summoner-level row of APG Table 2–9. The rest is divided: Hit Dice (minimum 1), skill ranks, feats, armor bonus, the Strength bonus, the Dexterity bonus, evolution points and maximum natural attacks. Each eidolon records its eight shares as `companionRelationship.sources[].allocatedComponents`. Its actual Hit Dice, HP from Constitution, skill-rank cap, feat budget, rank budget, armor bonus and its split, Strength and Dexterity bonuses, evolution pool and attack limit come from those shares. Explicit budgets take precedence over the ordinary per-HD formulas: the general feat slot is the allocated feats (plus any ordinary feats from the creature's own Class Levels), not half the allocated HD. The published 2nd-level example holds: each eidolon has 1 HD and BAB +2, good saves +3 and a bad save +0, and the eidolon given no feat has a general feat budget of 0 although it has 1 HD.

These readings are the chosen interpretation where the text is silent:

- The Str/Dex bonus divides into a Strength and a Dexterity budget, each the table's value, as the example gives +1 Strength to one eidolon and +1 Dexterity to the other.
- The brood's divided rank budget is the table's Skills column, 4 per HD: 6 + the base forms' Int 7 modifier (`eidolonTableSkillRanksPerHitDie`). APG Table 2–9 says "Eidolons with Intelligence scores above the base value modify these totals as normal (an eidolon receives a number of skill ranks equal to 6 + its Intelligence modifier per HD)", and an ordinary eidolon (#409) calculates `HD × max(1, 6 + Int modifier)`, which equals the column at Int 7. So each brood eidolon's rank budget is its allocated ranks plus its own Int adjustment per allocated HD: `allocated + HD × (max(1, 6 + Int modifier) − 4)`, never below 0. An Int 13 eidolon given 2 HD and 8 ranks has 14; an Int 7 one has its 8. The adjustment is the eidolon's own, so it never changes the brood's budget or another eidolon's share.
- The Special column's ability score increases and features (evasion, devotion, Multiattack and so on) are eidolon abilities, not divided base statistics, so each eidolon gains them by summoner level.

A missing share leaves the eidolon `unresolved` with a `companionComponentMissing` warning naming the missing components; nothing becomes zero. A share below 1 HD warns with `broodEidolonHitDice` and keeps calculating. The calculated `brood` result lists the recorded shares, missing components, the whole brood's budget, the sibling totals, over-budget components, group purchases and the largest size the purchases allow; `strengthDexterityBonus` is null for a brood eidolon. The contribution has `kind: 'component'` with `components { allocated, budget, pool }`; the source supplies the whole summoner level as `effectiveLevel`.

**Group purchases.** Larger Brood buys the large evolution (4 points, summoner level 8) and then the huge evolution (6 more, level 13, after large) once for the whole brood, spent "before he assigns evolution points to his individual eidolons": an 8th-level broodmaster with 11 points has 7 to divide. Group purchases are recorded with the budget on the associated Character's base entry as `companionGroupPurchases: [{ ruleKind, purchases }]`, never on an eidolon. A purchase below its level or without the large evolution keeps costing points and warns with `companionGroupPurchase`. Each eidolon still buys its own evolutions from its allocated points. The brood starts as two Small eidolons; the large evolution allows two Medium, four Small, or one Medium and two Small, and the huge evolution two Large or "any similar combination in which two smaller eidolons count as one eidolon of one size larger". The pool counts these places in Small eidolons (2, then 4, then 8) against the recorded eidolon sizes. An eidolon larger than its purchases allow warns with `broodEidolonSize`.

Once the brood holds the huge purchase, a brood eidolon records the large evolution to be Large, and the brood has already paid for it: the evolution costs nothing from the eidolon's allocated points, its row has `paidByGroup: true` and the sheet shows "Paid by the brood". Its Large size effects (+8 Str, +4 Con, −2 Dex, +2 natural armor, size skill adjustments) apply exactly as the large evolution's do, and it still takes four Small-eidolon places. The Huge upgrade is beyond the brood's purchases, so it costs its own 6 points and warns. Without the huge purchase, a brood eidolon's large evolution costs its 4 points and warns with `broodEidolonSize`, as before. Group purchases are disclosed only with access to the summoner. Without that access, an eidolon that records the large evolution still gets its correct points: the read checks the summoner's purchases on the server and passes only `isLargePaidByGroup: true` on the source and its component contribution when they pay for it, never the purchases, the budget or the brood's totals. An eidolon without the large evolution receives nothing. `listAllocationPools` reads each member's size with the resolver's own `eidolonSize`.

**No trimming, no winner.** Allocations above the brood's budget keep calculating and raise `companionComponentBudget` on every counting eidolon, naming each exceeded component. A summoner level drop shrinks the budget and keeps every share; disabling or losing the Broodmaster source makes it stop counting while its shares stay recorded for restoration, and a surviving ordinary summoner source advances an ordinary eidolon meanwhile. The text has no rule for redistributing a banished eidolon's shares, so none is invented.

**Disclosure.** An eidolon's own shares are on its relationship and calculate without access to the summoner. The brood's budget, the sibling totals and group purchases (`componentPool`) are disclosed only with access to the associated Character. To count the brood's places, `listAllocationPools` reads every member eidolon's base entry, including eidolons the viewer cannot open; only the aggregated `capacity.used` leaves the server, never an inaccessible eidolon's size or choices. A hidden summoner leaves the summoner level unresolved until a saved fallback supplies it; the budget then stays unresolved. The only group-purchase fact a reader without access receives is `isLargePaidByGroup`, described above.

**Writers and reads.** `companionRelationships.setSourceAllocation` now takes either `levels` or `components` (a partial set of whole numbers from 0 to 99, or null to clear). It refuses a share naming both, and components on a source that does not divide them. The new legacy Character writer `companionRelationships.setGroupPurchases({ characterId, ruleKind, purchases, operationId })` records one budget's purchases on the budget owner's sheet with write access to that Character, prunes Accepted Warnings and records the sheet change. `listAllocationPools` returns a `component` pool with the whole budget, allocated and remaining components, over-budget components, group purchases with their issues, brood places and each member's shares; inaccessible eidolons appear as "Character unavailable".

**Client.** `useCharacterCompanionAllocations` adds a component editor from `useComponentEditor` (`openComponentEditor`, `componentForm`, `submitComponents`, `clearComponents`) that records all eight shares together, prefilled from the recorded share or the brood's remaining budget, and `setGroupPurchase`. `buildCompanionAllocationPoolView` words the component pool. The existing pool block shows it read-only; the editors are handed off through the #419 UI contract.

Representative browse-only seeds add the Broodmaster Archetype with Eidolon Brood (2nd) and Larger Brood (8th) ([representative archetype notes](../catalog-import/archetypes.md)). Open questions: the PRD's "At 2nd level" leaves a 1st-level broodmaster's eidolon unstated, and a 1-HD row simply warns that two eidolons exceed it; Brood Link, Bond Senses, Shield Ally, Maker's Call, Transposition, Brood Bond and Merge Forms remain prose.

### Prepared reduced progression (#420)

Master Summoner's Lesser Eidolon halves the summoner level before the eidolon table is read. This is a reduced progression: one source transforms the class level it reads, with no budget and no player division, unlike the level allocation of #410 and the component allocation of #419. The actual summoner level stays a separate value.

[The cited rule declaration](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-companion-rules.ts) curates Master Summoner as the `masterSummonerEidolon` supporting rule kind, from Ultimate Magic p. 80 ([legacy PRD summoner archetypes](<https://legacy.aonprd.com/ultimateMagic/spellcastingClassOptions/summoner.html#master-summoner-(archetype)>); the PRD prints no page, so the page follows Archives of Nethys' source line). The text was checked against both: "A master summoner's class level is halved (minimum 1) for the purposes of determining his eidolon's abilities, Hit Dice, evolution pool, and so on. The eidolon otherwise functions as normal. This ability replaces the summoner's normal eidolon ability." The ticket names the APG; the archetype is in Ultimate Magic. A summoner source gets this rule kind from the Master Summoner Selection, its Lesser Eidolon Grant, or an original summoner Class Level with the Archetype active.

**The reduction contract.** A rule's `reduction` declares a label, the feature and table it applies to, the class family it reads and ordered `steps`: `divide` (rounding down, as the CRB's general rounding rule says) and `minimum`. Master Summoner's steps are divide by 2, then minimum 1. `reduceProgressionLevel` in `src/lib/character-sheet-companion-sources.ts` applies them; a level below 1 stays as it is, because the minimum is a held summoner level's. Further admitted variants that transform an input level reuse the declaration with their own steps.

**Operation order.** Each step names its input:

1. Read the named input `classLevels` for the original summoner family: the associated Character's actual summoner Class Levels, through the #317 linked input. A saved fallback for that input is a summoner level, so it is halved like a calculated one.
2. Count only original summoner sources (#418). An Unchained eidolon never counts a Master Summoner source.
3. Claim the summoner family once. A counting reduced source replaces the ordinary contribution of its family whatever the source order, so the whole level never counts beside the halved one.
4. Apply the reduction steps in order: halve, rounding down, then at least 1. Summoner 7 gives 3; summoner 1, 2 and 3 give 1; summoner 20 gives 10.
5. Combine the counting contributions and look up APG Table 2–9 once at the reduced level.
6. Everything that reads "summoner level" for the eidolon reads the reduced level: Hit Dice, BAB, saves, Feats, armor, Str/Dex bonus, evolution pool, maximum attacks, the Special column (ability score increases at 5, 10 and 15, evasion, devotion, Multiattack), the evolutions' summoner-level minimums and per-level limits (improved natural armor per 5 levels, an ability increase per score per 6 levels). Reading the evolution requirements at the reduced level is the chosen interpretation of "abilities … and so on".
7. Nothing else is reduced. The eidolon's ranks are still 6 + its own Int modifier per (reduced) HD, its HP the recorded plain number plus Constitution per HD, and the Feats column its feat budget. No fixed companion budget is invented and no Int adjustment is added to one.

At summoner level 7 the eidolon has 3 HD, BAB +3 and 5 evolution points, against 6 HD and 10 points for an ordinary 7th-level summoner's eidolon (#248). The summoner keeps its 7 Class Levels; the eidolon's actual Hit Dice, and its Militia Character Facts, are the reduced table's 3.

The contribution has `kind: 'reduced'`, `classLevels` (the actual level read), `effectiveLevel` (the reduced level) and `reduction.steps`, each `{ kind, operand, result }`. The eidolon's `sources` add the Ultimate Magic citation and its `restrictions` the rule's text while the source counts.

**Changes, loss and restoration.** A summoner level loss reduces again from the new level and keeps every evolution, which warns when over the smaller pool or below a requirement; restoring the level restores the same results. Turning Master Summoner off makes the source an ordinary summoner source with the whole row; turning it back on restores the reduced results. Losing every source leaves the eidolon dormant with its choices. A hidden summoner leaves the summoner level unresolved; a saved fallback supplies it, and restored access suspends the fallback.

**Competing rules.** Broodmaster and Master Summoner each replace the summoner's eidolon, and no published rule orders them. When both apply to one source, through a Class Level with both active or one active Archetype's Selection or Grant while the other is active, no rule is chosen. A source supplied by a turned-off Archetype's Selection keeps that Archetype's rule kind without counting and joins no conflict. `deriveCompanionSourceRules` gives the source the ordinary `eidolon` kind and lists both in `ruleConflicts`, stored with the source's rule kind by the relationship writers so a reader without access to the summoner sees the conflict too. The sheet read passes the conflict to the calculation as `ruleConflict` on the source and its contribution: `{ disclosure: 'disclosed', ruleKinds }` or `{ disclosure: 'undisclosed' }`, and absent without a conflict. Like `unsupportedArchetypes`, the competing rules name the summoner's Archetypes, so they are disclosed only with access to the associated Character. Without it the read passes `{ disclosure: 'undisclosed' }`; the eidolon is unresolved in the same way, a saved summoner level fallback does not resolve it, and the warning and the view say that rules on the summoner compete without naming them. The summoner's name and levels stay hidden too. The contribution is an ordinary `eidolon` contribution whatever rule kind its source names; it keeps its summoner level but has no effective level, so the eidolon is `unresolved` with a `companionProgressionRuleConflict` warning naming both rules. Every recorded choice stays, including a Broodmaster share, which cannot be rewritten while no component rule applies. Turning one Archetype off resolves the conflict and restores the other rule's results. A saved player interpretation between the two rules is not offered: the conflict is the archetype incompatibility itself.

**Client.** `buildEidolonProgressionView` adds `reduction` (`label`, the actual `summonerLevel`, the `tableLevel` and the ordered `steps`) and `ruleConflict` (`labels`, `summary`; no labels while the rules are undisclosed). A reduced contribution's `allocationSummary` words the operation trail, its source label is "Lesser eidolon", and a conflicting contribution's value reads "Competing rules". JSX is handed off through the #420 UI contract.

Representative browse-only seeds add the Master Summoner Archetype with Lesser Eidolon (1st), Summoning Mastery (1st) and Augment Summoning (2nd) ([representative archetype notes](../catalog-import/archetypes.md)). There is no new writer and no new read. Summoner levels raised or lost on the summoner's sheet republish the eidolon's Militia Character Facts in the same change (#423). Open question: Summoning Mastery and Augment Summoning remain prose.

### Prepared Combined Form (#421)

A Synthesist wears its eidolon as living armor. The summoner and the eidolon stay two Characters with their own builds; the summoner's sheet offers a read-only **Combined Form** calculated from both on each read. Nothing is stored, so viewing it declares no current fusion, summoning, damage or duration, and changes no combat state, roster, officer, Militia Character Fact or relationship.

[The cited rule declaration](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-companion-rules.ts) curates Synthesist's Fused Eidolon as the `synthesistEidolon` supporting rule kind, from Ultimate Magic pp. 80–81 ([legacy PRD summoner archetypes](<https://legacy.aonprd.com/ultimateMagic/spellcastingClassOptions/summoner.html#synthesist-(archetype)>)). The PRD prints no page; the official FAQ cites the archetype at p. 80 and Archives of Nethys' source line p. 81. A summoner source gets this rule kind from the Synthesist Selection, its Fused Eidolon Grant, or an original summoner Class Level with the Archetype active. "In all other cases, this ability functions as the summoner's normal eidolon ability", so the eidolon progresses at the whole summoner level, as an ordinary eidolon; its sources and restrictions cite the archetype. Fused Eidolon replaces the eidolon ability, so Synthesist competes with Broodmaster and Master Summoner like they compete with each other (#420): with no published precedence, the eidolon is unresolved and so is the Combined Form.

**Fused statistics.** `src/lib/character-sheet-combined-form.ts` recalculates the summoner's own sheet with the eidolon's contributions substituted, so stacking, armor, size and every derived statistic follow the ordinary resolver. Each value names the build it comes from.

These readings are the chosen interpretation where the text is silent: the eidolon's physical scores replace the summoner's entirely, including the summoner's own items' and race's bonuses to them; the eidolon's size replaces the summoner's own size adjustments; and the fused hit points use the eidolon's Constitution with the summoner's own Hit Dice. Fused Eidolon requires the eidolon to be "at least the same size as the synthesist" (UM p. 80); a smaller eidolon is shown as an advisory and the form is still calculated. The eidolon's natural attack count and maximum are shown, but attacks while fused, Attack Routines and the Fused Link, Maker's Jump and Split Forms features are not calculated, and the view says so.

**Unresolved and fallback values.** Without a Synthesist Archetype active on original summoner levels there is no Combined Form. The view is unresolved, with a reason, when no eidolon is linked; when the eidolon is inaccessible or its relationship is not active, which discloses no name, link or value; when more than one eidolon is active; when the eidolon's progression is unresolved, including competing rules; and on Unchained summoner levels, whose Combined Form is never assumed. An unresolved view keeps the summoner's own mental scores and leaves every eidolon-dependent value null. Unrecorded eidolon hit points leave only the temporary hit points unresolved. Only the eidolon's access check is read as unavailability: a failure to calculate an accessible eidolon is an error, never an unavailable eidolon. The eidolon's summoner-level inputs are listed with their resolution, so a saved fallback in use is visible.

**Read.** `characterSheetCombinedForm.read({ characterId })` authorizes the summoner like the sheet read, returns null for a Character that is not a Synthesist, and reads the eidolon only through its own access check. It has no writer and adds no table or index. `useCharacterCompanions(...).combinedForm` (`useCharacterCombinedForm`) subscribes to it, and `buildCombinedFormView` words each value with its source, the unresolved reasons and a read-only notice. `CharacterCombinedForm` renders it inside the Companions section: the size and four combat numbers at a glance, and one disclosure opening the abilities, every statistic with its breakdown, skills, evolutions with the natural attack count, linked values, features and rules.

Representative browse-only seeds add the Synthesist Archetype with its six features ([representative archetype notes](../catalog-import/archetypes.md)).

### Prepared cohorts (#417)

A cohort is an ordinary Character with its own Class Levels, sheet and Character Owner, linked to its leader by a `cohort` Companion Relationship. Linking an existing Character keeps its Class Levels; nothing is trimmed, invented or replaced. This slice calculates the cohort's **Cohort Equivalence** and the level its leader allows. It adds no Monsters as PCs system: no Challenge Rating, no level adjustment and no racial-HD advancement.

[The cited cohort catalog](../../src/lib/catalog/representative-cohorts.ts) curates CRB p. 129's Leadership rules, checked against the [legacy PRD feat text](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/feats.html#leadership): the Table: Leadership cohort levels, the reputation modifiers and the cohort modifiers. It also curates the complete Bestiary and Bestiary 2 Monster Cohorts tables, 29 creatures, checked against the [legacy PRD Monster Cohorts page](https://legacy.aonprd.com/bestiary/monsterCohorts.html). That page prints no page numbers and no primary page source was reachable, so these citations name the book only; the Bestiary page is unverified. The young dragon is not curated: its published level is its Challenge Rating + 8 and the sheet records no Challenge Rating, so a dragon uses an entered equivalence. Later Bestiaries, Ultimate Campaign and Animal Archive tables are not curated.

**Cohort Equivalence.** `base.state.cohortEquivalence` on the cohort records the monster basis; no record is an ordinary cohort.

| Recorded                                     | Equivalence                                     |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Nothing, no Racial Hit Dice                  | The cohort's Class Levels                       |
| Nothing, with Racial Hit Dice                | Missing: `cohortEquivalenceMissing`, unresolved |
| `{ kind: 'published', creatureKey }`         | The published cohort level + its Class Levels   |
| `{ kind: 'entered', creature, level }`       | The entered level + its Class Levels            |
| `{ kind: 'entered', creature, level: null }` | Missing: `cohortEquivalenceMissing`, unresolved |

The Bestiary says a monster cohort "should generally gain a key class level" when it advances, so its later Class Levels add to the mapping: a pegasus (cohort level 6, four racial HD) with one fighter level has equivalence 7, actual Hit Dice 5 and character level 1. Actual Hit Dice, Class Levels and Cohort Equivalence stay separate, and Militia Character Facts never read the equivalence.

**The leader's limit.** The cohort rule reads two named linked inputs from the leader: `characterLevel` and the new `abilityModifier` input for Charisma, each through the #317 seam with its current and permanent projections, saved fallbacks and interpretations. The Leadership Score is level + Charisma modifier + the recorded modifiers. The table gives the score's cohort level (0 for a score of 1 or lower, 17th from 24), and "you can only recruit a cohort who is two or more levels lower than yourself" gives the leader's level − 2. CRB p. 129 consults the table only when a cohort is attracted, and only the second limit persists after recruitment, so the cohort's allowed level is the leader's level − 2. The table level is shown as information, as what the current score would attract now; it never warns. A new cohort could be attracted at the lower of the two. These inputs feed only the cohort calculation: the cohort's own prerequisites and statistics read its own sheet, never the leader's level.

The Leadership modifiers are recorded on the cohort relationship's supporting source as `sources[].leadershipModifiers`: the reputation modifiers chosen from the cohort's point of view, the cohort modifiers (a familiar, special mount or animal companion at recruitment; a different alignment; −2 per cohort killed) and an `other` whole number from −20 to 20 for rules outside the CRB table. No record applies none. Counting sources that record different modifiers are a conflict: the score is unresolved and no source wins by order.

`calculateCharacterSheet(...).cohort` returns `status` (`active` with a counting source, `dormant` without one, `unlinked` for a record on a Character with no cohort relationship), `equivalence` (basis, creature, its published or entered level, Class Levels, value, missing state and citation), `leadership` (each leader input with its resolution, itemized modifiers, score, table level, leader limit, the level a cohort could be attracted at now and conflict) and `overLevel`, true when the cohort is not two or more levels below its leader. A cohort that is not two or more levels below its leader warns with `cohortOverLevel` and keeps calculating. A cohort above the table level, for example one that advanced through experience after the score fell, does not warn. A missing leader input warns with `cohortLeadershipUnresolved` and leaves the allowed level null rather than zero.

**Links, loss and access.** The cohort's sources count like every companion source. An interrupted link keeps counting with unavailable leader inputs, so the allowed level is unresolved until restoration or a saved fallback. A replaced link, a disabled source or a lost Leadership Selection makes the cohort `dormant`: its Class Levels, equivalence record and the source's Leadership modifiers all stay, and restoring the feat restores the same results. A hidden leader discloses nothing: its source key reads `rule:cohort`, and its name, entry, values and Leadership modifiers never appear. The modifiers describe the leader and are written with access to it, so a reader without that access gets no modifier rows or labels: the cohort input carries `areModifiersUndisclosed: true` with no source's `leadershipModifiers`, and the result has `leadership.areModifiersUndisclosed: true`. Saved fallbacks for the leader's level and Charisma modifier supply the level the leader allows and the over-level check. The Leadership Score and the table level need the modifiers, so they stay unresolved rather than wrong; `cohortLeadershipUnresolved` warns only while a leader input is missing or the sources conflict. The cohort's view lists hidden modifiers as "Unavailable", keeping "None" for an accessible leader with no recorded modifiers. Restored access shows the modifiers and the score again.

**Writers and reads.** `characterSheet.setCohortEquivalence({ ...scope, equivalence: record | null, operationId })` is a new legacy Character writer with write access to the cohort. It refuses levels outside 1–30, fractions, and creature names that are empty or longer than 64 characters, records the sheet change and prunes Accepted Warnings. `companionRelationships.setSourceAllocation` now also takes `leadership: modifiers | null`, with write access to the leader; it refuses malformed modifiers, a write naming more than one kind of choice, and modifiers on a source that does not support a cohort. Public sheet reads return the `cohort` result in both projections and `companionCalculationInputs` as `{ kind: 'cohort', cohort }` for the Situation preview, never empty progression arrays; `companionRelationships.list` returns each source's `leadershipModifiers`.

**Client.** `useCharacterCompanions(...).cohort` (`useCharacterCohort`) returns `buildCohortView(snapshot)`, the curated creature and modifier choices, an equivalence editor and, on the leader's sheet, one Leadership modifier editor per cohort source with `buildLeadershipSourcesView` previews from the leader's own sheet. Both editors use `react-hook-form` and `zod` with distinct empty and malformed messages, keep refused drafts, give local save feedback and disable writes during maintenance. JSX is handed off through the #417 UI contract.

Representative browse-only seeds add the Leadership feat (CRB p. 129, character level 7th). Open questions: followers are not modelled; the familiar, special mount or animal companion modifier is recorded by the player as a fact at recruitment rather than derived from the leader's current relationships; the Bestiary page numbers remain unverified.

### Movement and publication

- Moving a Character includes its active Companions with the same Character Owner, recursively: a Character, its cohort and that cohort's familiar move together if those conditions hold throughout. Stop at inactive links or different owners. Moving a Companion alone does not pull its associated Character along. A differently owned Companion may stay behind, with the separated relationship retained inactive.
- Apply the existing departure, homebrew-preservation and publication contract to the whole moving group. Prepare and publish every included Character's campaign transition, preserved sheet, relationship changes and departure effects together; no partial group move may become visible. Revalidate current owners, links, access and inputs at publication, reconciling concurrent changes instead of overwriting them.
- These are implementation requirements derived from grouped movement, not new departure authority. Existing automatic access revocation, deletion and recovery policies still apply. Recalculate affected Militia Character Facts coherently with the change; only changed facts invalidate their prior review, alongside the existing roster/departure review rules. Frozen Resolution Records remain unchanged.

### Interrupted calculation and militia participation

- When a relationship becomes inactive, apply any explicit rules for the remaining statistics. Where those rules are silent, associated-Character-dependent values are unresolved until explicitly adjusted; preserve independent statistics and all choices. Never silently freeze prior totals or substitute zero.
- An explicit fallback contributes while normal calculation is unavailable. Once calculation resumes, the fallback stops contributing automatically but stays saved for another interruption. Deliberate lasting changes use ordinary sheet adjustments separately.
- Militia participation is opt-in under the existing roster and officer rules. Creating a Companion does not add it to the roster or assign an officer role.
- Militia Character Facts read each Character's ordinary permanent statistics. Viewing a Synthesist's fused form never changes the summoner's or eidolon's militia contribution; deliberate table rulings use the existing adjustments. Permanent-only filtering applies transitively to linked dependencies.
- The militia reads actual Hit Dice, including Companion Progression where applicable, never a familiar's effective-HD substitution, an effective progression level or Cohort Equivalence. The roster Hit Dice override still replaces that value, including an override of zero. Applying actual Hit Dice to a familiar serving as an Officer is the product's chosen reading; the militia corpus does not definitively resolve that interaction.
- An unresolved value actually required to calculate the militia week must be supplied before Weekly Confirmation. Keep the Companion on the roster and allow editing; unrelated unresolved statistics do not block confirmation. Do not omit a required contribution, use zero or reuse stale values.

### Ordinary companion facts in militia weeks (#423)

**Refresh with the change.** Every ordinary edit to a Character, its local or campaign definitions, its relationships or their sources, and every Character move, recalculates the Militia Character Facts of each Companion downstream of it in the same mutation: its Companions, theirs and so on, across every relationship kind. The traversal follows only links whose Companion still reads the change: active links, interrupted links whose supporting sources still count (a retained link after a master leaves a campaign, for example), and any link the change itself writes, such as a source switched off or a Companion just replaced. Replaced history and links with no counting source are skipped. Each refresh uses the trusted, viewer-independent permanent calculation, so facts never depend on who edits. Equal facts write nothing: neither the live projection, the stored ledger facts nor the canonical revision changes, so a reviewed week stays reviewed. Changed facts advance the revision and stale any review taken before them.

**Traversal bound.** One change refreshes at most 16 downstream Characters (`maxCompanionFactRefreshes` in `convex/lib/companionRelationships.ts`). Each refresh loads the Companion's prepared sheet and its associated Character's sheet in the same transaction, so the bound keeps a change well inside Convex's transaction read limits; the relationship graph's own 1,024-row limit is far too large for that. Retained history never counts toward it. A change with more downstream Characters is refused with "This Companion group is too large to change at once. Switch off the supporting sources of a Companion you no longer use, then try again." and changes nothing, rather than leaving stale facts; a Companion whose sources no longer count leaves the traversal after that switch. Representative companion groups are far smaller (a brood of up to six, a familiar, a cohort with its own Companion). The bound of 16 is revisited once the read budget per refresh is measured.

**What the facts read.** Facts read the permanent calculation: character level, actual Hit Dice (`actualHitDice` with Companion Progression), racial Hit Dice and the six permanent scores. They never read familiar effective HD, a progression level, Cohort Equivalence or a viewed Combined Form. Permanent filtering follows linked inputs: a short Spell Effect on the associated Character neither changes a Companion's scores nor counts as its supporting source in the permanent projection. The roster's Hit Dice override, zero included, still replaces actual Hit Dice.

**Unresolved scores.** A score the sheet can't calculate yet, such as the Strength, Dexterity and Constitution of a dormant or unsupported Companion, is listed in the facts' optional `unresolvedAbilities` (omitted when every score is known, so established facts and revisions are unchanged; an empty list reads the same, in both the snapshot schema and the Convex validators). The listed score itself is the partial total and is never read as known. The same field is stored in the live snapshot, Setup options, the ledger facts, `character.sheetPermanentFacts` and the Character lists, and frozen Resolution Records keep it as recorded.

**What blocks Confirmation.** Only a value this week's rules read is required. An Ambassador, Spymaster or Marshal whose role abilities include an unresolved score adds a `<checkId>:officer:<characterId>:unresolved-<ability>` requirement to each Loyalty, Secrecy or Security check the week makes; an Overseer adds `<checkId>:overseer:<characterId>:unresolved-<ability>` to the check they support. From Ruleset Version 10, a commandant whose actual Hit Dice are unresolved and who has no roster override adds `<choiceId>:commandant:<characterId>:unresolved-hit-dice` to a Drill; Version 9 still reads level. A team manager whose Charisma is unresolved adds `<checkId>:manager:<characterId>:unresolved-charisma` to each check that adds their team's manager bonus, although no current Companion calculation leaves Charisma unresolved. Such a check's total stays unknown in the preview, so a partial score never decides an outcome. A roster member without a role, or an officer whose unresolved scores no check this week reads, never blocks. Roster membership, officer assignment and sheet editing stay available throughout. These are calculation-readiness requirements rather than a rules change, so they need no Ruleset Version.

**Officer cards and Assign.** An Ambassador, Spymaster or Marshal whose role abilities include an unresolved score reads "Unresolved" on its card and is compared with no other holder; a resolved holder's bonus still applies. With no resolved holder, the effect names what the bonus needs, such as "Security bonus needs Strength", as the commandant card does for Hit Dice. The Assign picker previews such a candidate as "Str unresolved" rather than a bonus. Neither ever shows the partial score or +0.

**One boundary.** Setup options and `requireReviewedCharacters`, Militia Corrections, the ledger, previews and Confirmation all read the same selected facts. A public History Rewrite publication command is still not registered; when it is, it must read these facts through `selectedCanonicalMilitiaState` and leave frozen records as recorded. Viewing a Combined Form stays read-only.

**Catalog Releases (#712).** A release calculates a Companion's candidate facts with every associated Character upstream read under the same candidate release, so they never mix releases. Equal facts keep their generation; only a campaign whose facts changed stales its review. Every write above that refreshes a Companion also marks its candidate work dirty, and a worker that read earlier inputs cannot complete. See [Linked Companions, moves and keyed lists](../catalog-import/releases.md#linked-companions-moves-and-keyed-lists).
