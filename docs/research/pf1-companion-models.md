# PF1 companion progression and combined-form models

Research for [Identify companion progression and combined-form models](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/248), supporting [Decide companion progression and cross-sheet calculations](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/249). Investigated 2026-10-02. This extends [the companion rules and catalog research](pf1-companion-rules.md); it does not replace its pinned-content audit.

## Scope and settled inputs

The question is which **different calculations** the six representative patterns require, not how to store or implement them. Sources are the Core Rulebook (CRB), Advanced Player’s Guide (APG), Ultimate Magic, and a narrowly flagged Bestiary section, read through the official rules reference. Examples below are calculations from those texts unless described as published examples. This is not an exhaustive archetype inventory, compatibility survey, or implementation catalog.

Apply the resolution of [Decide how companions fit the Character Sheet](https://github.com/AndreasUnunger/EverythingPath/issues/246): one retained linked Character identity per companion, independent ownership, one active associated Character, multiple companions and compatible sources, retained inactive relationships, advisory rules validation, and unresolved dependent statistics with temporary manual fallbacks. The witch keeps **one unchanged spell collection on the witch’s sheet**, including after familiar replacement. These are settled product inputs, not propositions for this research to reopen. Combat damage, summoning status, timers, and daily usage remain outside this builder’s scope.

## Contrasting examples

### Combined sources: one progression does not mean one universal level

A druid 3/ranger 4 supporting a wolf has effective druid level **4**, calculated as 3 + (4 − 3). The animal table gives **4 actual d8 HD**, while the associated Character has seven class levels. Ranger’s restricted species list and its companion benefits do not become seven-level druid features merely because advancement combines. [CRB druid and companion table](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/classes/druid.html#animal-companions-184), [CRB ranger, Hunter’s Bond](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/classes/ranger.html#hunter-s-bond).

A witch 2/wizard 3/fighter 2 with both familiar grants uses combined familiar progression **5**. Witch precedence is explicit; the familiar stores witch spells rather than the wizard’s spellbook contents. The ordinary familiar rules separately reference the master’s total character level for effective HD, class-derived BAB/base saves, HP excluding temporary HP, and recorded skill ranks. Its normal creature HD do not become five familiar “class levels.” [APG Witch’s Familiar](https://legacy.aonprd.com/advancedPlayersGuide/baseClasses/witch.html#witchs-familiar), [CRB familiars](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/classes/wizard.html#familiars).

The design implication is an explicit distinction between a progression’s contributing sources and each statistic’s dependencies. Neither summing every class level nor importing the associated sheet’s final attack/save bonuses reproduces these rules. Witch precedence also supplies evidence for a rule-specific winner; it does not establish a general “newest source wins” policy.

### Beast Master: divide the input level

A level-7 Beast Master has four effective druid levels. Allocating all four to one companion gives **4 HD**; allocating two to each of two companions gives **3 HD each**. The effective-level budget is conserved; the resulting HD total is not. At ranger level 12, Strong Bond makes the total budget equal ranger level, allowing the added levels to be assigned immediately. [APG Beast Master](https://legacy.aonprd.com/advancedPlayersGuide/coreClasses/ranger.html#beast-master), [CRB animal companion table](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/classes/druid.html#animal-companions-184).

Consequently, retaining only a companion’s final HD would lose the choice that produced it. Conversely, treating multiple animals as shares of a fixed HD total would calculate the wrong progression. This example does not decide how another granting class’s contribution interacts with an existing Beast Master allocation: the reviewed passages do not explicitly work through that combination’s allocation order.

### Broodmaster: divide the outputs, preserve exceptions

The published level-2 example gives each eidolon **1 HD**, but **BAB +2**, good saves **+3**, and poor save **+0**. It gives one a feat and the other none, despite equal HD. Its Strength increase goes to one and Dexterity increase to the other. Which saves are good still follows each base form. These results cannot be reconstructed by treating each as an ordinary level-1 eidolon. [Broodmaster](https://aonprd.com/ArchetypeDisplay.aspx?FixedName=Summoner%20Broodmaster), [APG eidolon base forms](https://legacy.aonprd.com/advancedPlayersGuide/baseClasses/summoner.html#base-forms).

Larger Brood adds another ordering distinction: at summoner level 8, spend four of eleven evolution points on the brood’s size allowance **before** distributing the remaining seven to individual eidolons. This is a group purchase, unlike each creature’s separately purchased evolutions. [Ultimate Magic, Larger Brood](https://legacy.aonprd.com/ultimateMagic/spellcastingClassOptions/summoner.html#larger-brood).

The evidence therefore requires independently accountable component budgets, plus results expressly exempt from division. It does not support an assumption that every number on a creature’s sheet follows its allocated HD. The normal table remains useful, but applying all its relationships independently to each recipient would erase the archetype’s exceptions.

### Master Summoner: transform the progression input

At summoner level 7, Lesser Eidolon uses effective level **3**: halve seven and round down. The original eidolon table then gives **3 HD and five evolution points**, versus **6 HD and ten points** for an ordinary level-7 summoner’s eidolon. The minimum makes level 1 use effective level 1. The archetype halves the input for eidolon abilities, HD, pool, and related calculations; it does not halve the summoner’s actual class levels. [Master Summoner](https://aonprd.com/ArchetypeDisplay.aspx?FixedName=Summoner%20Master%20Summoner), [CRB rounding rule](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/gettingStarted.html#playing-the-game), [APG eidolon table](https://legacy.aonprd.com/advancedPlayersGuide/baseClasses/summoner.html#eidolons).

This differs from both allocation models: no player chooses how much of the budget this eidolon receives. Whether a shared calculation interface expresses that transformation as a modifier, a distinct progression, or another construct remains a design choice. These examples concern the original summoner; they do not establish archetype compatibility with the unchained version.

### Synthesist: calculate a combined form without merging records

While fused, eidolon Strength/Dexterity/Constitution replace the summoner’s, while the summoner retains Intelligence/Wisdom/Charisma. Eidolon HP become temporary HP; its BAB, armor/natural armor, abilities, and evolutions supply other specified benefits. The summoner’s armor ceases to benefit the fused form, and the eidolon has no independent skills or feats. [Synthesist, Fused Eidolon](https://aonprd.com/ArchetypeDisplay.aspx?FixedName=Summoner%20Synthesist).

For example, a summoner with Strength 10 and Wisdom 14, fused with an eidolon whose resulting Strength is 16, uses Strength **16**, not 26, and Wisdom **14**. This is substitution of selected inputs, not addition of two entire sheets. The rule does not turn temporary HP into permanent summoner HD or ordinary class levels. A static calculated form can expose those distinctions without asserting that fusion is currently active.

At level 16, Split Forms expressly has the eidolon function normally for its duration. This prevents treating “has no independent feats/skills” as an irreversible deletion of all separate-form build information. What choices a builder requests for this later form is still a product decision. [Ultimate Magic, Split Forms](https://legacy.aonprd.com/ultimateMagic/spellcastingClassOptions/summoner.html#split-forms).

### Monster cohorts: equivalence is not creature advancement

The Bestiary lists a pegasus at **cohort level 6**, while its creature entry has **four racial HD** and CR 3. If it subsequently gains one fighter level through cohort advancement, the resulting example has five actual HD, one class level, and cohort equivalence seven. None of the values is interchangeable. [Monster cohorts](https://legacy.aonprd.com/bestiary/monsterCohorts.html), [pegasus statblock](https://legacy.aonprd.com/bestiary/pegasus.html).

Leadership limits initial recruitment both through its table and to at least two levels below the leader. Applying that ceiling directly to a monster’s HD would therefore answer a different question from applying published cohort equivalence. The Bestiary also uses CR + 8 for young dragons, without thereby adopting the separate monster-PC advancement subsystem. [CRB Leadership](https://legacy.aonprd.com/coreRulebook/feats.html#leadership), [Bestiary monster cohorts](https://legacy.aonprd.com/bestiary/monsterCohorts.html).

## Lifecycle boundaries and remaining decisions

The examples establish meaningful calculation distinctions. They do **not** decide whether the application implements six independent calculators or composes common operations. A candidate contract can be assessed against the concrete examples above; it must preserve their different inputs, allocations, substitutions, and exceptions.

For partial source loss, no reviewed source supplies a general algorithm to trim allocations, choose which recipient loses HD, undo a group purchase, or restore previous choices after access returns. Beast Master’s explicit release/death permission is narrower than “any unavailable source.” Broodmaster banishment is not an explicit authorization to redistribute the absent eidolon’s allocations. These absences leave application behavior to the pending decision; they do not authorize freezing old results or inventing zeroes under the already-settled fallback policy.

Familiar dismissal has a specific exception to that silence: the [Paizo CRB FAQ](https://paizo.com/paizo/faq/v5748nruor1fm) says the dismissed familiar returns to a normal creature. That does not define arbitrary loss of just one multiclass grant. Witch replacement text specifies a new starting spell complement, but the accepted unchanged collection on the witch’s sheet remains the product rule. [APG Witch’s Familiar](https://legacy.aonprd.com/advancedPlayersGuide/baseClasses/witch.html#witchs-familiar).

The remaining design questions are bounded:

- **Allocation provenance and corrections:** how do retained choices explain which source supplied each budget, and what becomes unresolved when a decreased budget no longer fits? Decide explicit user correction versus any automatic adjustment; the sources do not choose a recipient to penalize.
- **Precedence and compatibility:** represent explicit witch precedence separately from combinations with no documented ordering. The six examples do not establish a universal order for stacking reductions, bonuses, source combination, and allocation.
- **Combined-form presentation:** determine which static results are visible on each linked sheet and which result feeds Militia Character Facts. Retain both identities. No play-time fusion switch, timer, damage tracker, or automatic double participation follows from these texts.
- **Cross-sheet projections:** familiars exclude temporary HP, but the cited sentence does not explicitly distinguish current from maximum HP. Choose the builder interpretation consistently with its lack of current-damage tracking. Keep the existing plain-number HP input and permanent-Int skill policy unless explicitly amended; specify the projection read by each dependency instead of silently importing a whole sheet.
- **Monster admission:** decide whether to admit published Bestiary cohort mappings and the necessary creature inputs. The legacy page includes Bestiaries 1–5, beyond a creature-type table. Unlisted equivalence suggestions are not a ready-made numeric catalog, and GM discretion does not add a new approval gate under this project’s policy.

## Evidence limits and follow-up boundary

The prior research’s pinned `pf1` v11.11 / `pf1-content` 11.4.0 findings remain the catalog evidence. This note adds no claim that those pins contain structured allocation, subtype, species, or combined-form definitions. Missing automation remains visible under the agreed catalog and manual-fallback contracts.

The CRB FAQ was available through the search index; direct Paizo FAQ fetches returned HTTP 403. The Ultimate Magic FAQ also returned 403, and targeted searches did not recover its primary text. Therefore this note **does not verify** reported Synthesist FAQ clarifications concerning multiclass BAB replacement, Skilled, or ability-increase interactions. Recovering those specific entries from an accessible official copy is a narrow remaining rules-verification task before finalizing those edge cases. Forum quotations were not treated as primary evidence.

Further work need not survey every archetype to make the pending design decision. Test the contract against these six examples first. If the decision requires stacking a named bonus with a named allocation/reduction rule, research that precise interaction and source order. A later content inventory can then enumerate admitted variants without pretending this representative matrix proves complete catalog coverage.
